\section{Errors on the Synthetic Testbed}\label{app:errors}
% Moved from the paper (section The Landscape, One Category) to fit the page limit. The paper no longer refers to this section.
The body methods were evaluated on a synthetic flat with a seated person, a desk and an exact ground truth.
In this flat, a virtual camera took 36 photos and three hand-held sweeps.
From a single photo, NLF (Neural Localizer Fields) predicts body points with a median error of 11\,cm.
With its 2D points triangulated over five photos, the median error is 3.2\,cm.
SAM~3D Body is as accurate from one photo (11.3\,cm).
For the body, the task is therefore close to solved.
However, these methods do not extend to other objects.

The table below gives the 3D error in centimetres: the median and the 90th percentile over the named points visible in the first photo, and the median over the occluded points.
Coverage is the share of points with an output.
The last two columns give the median error of two ergonomic measurements.
For people, these are the elbow angle and the trunk flexion, in degrees.
For furniture, these are the desk width and the eye-to-screen distance, in centimetres.
Configurations with several photos used simulated motion tracking, with an error of 0.5\,cm and 0.2$^\circ$ in the pose of each photo.
``True 2D'' replaces the first stage by the exact pixels, which isolates the geometry.
Robust triangulation leaves out the photos whose output disagrees with the others.
In the DINOv2 rows, the points were marked on example photos of another desk, or of the same one.
``A4'' denotes the four corners of an A4 sheet of paper on the desk, which fix the metric scale and the plane of the desk.
